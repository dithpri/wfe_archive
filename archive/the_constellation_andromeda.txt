Andromeda is one of the 88 modern constellations and was originally listed among the 48 constellations by Ptolemy in the 2nd century CE. Located in the northern celestial hemisphere, it is named for Andromeda, daughter of Cassiopeia, in the Greek myth, who was chained to a rock to be eaten by the sea monster Cetus.
