Caelum is a faint constellation in the southern sky, introduced in the 1750s by Nicolas Louis de Lacaille and counted among the 88 modern constellations. Its name means &quot;chisel&quot; in Latin, and it was formerly known as Caelum Sculptorium (&quot;Engraver&#39;s Chisel&quot;). The constellation&#39;s brightest star, Alpha Caeli, is only of magnitude 4.45, and only one other star, (Gamma) &amp;#947;1 Caeli, is brighter than magnitude 5.
