Orkneyjar is the Old Norse name for the Orkney Islands, an archipelago off the northern coast of mainland Scotland, which translates literally to &quot;seal islands&quot;.

Meaning and History

&#149; Etymology: Derived from the Proto-Celtic or Pictish root &amp;#632;orkos (often interpreted as &quot;pig&quot;) combined with the Old Norse suffix -eyjar meaning &quot;islands&quot;.

&#149; Norse Era: From the late 9th century until 1472, the islands were a prominent Norse earldom ruled by Scandinavian Jarls and deeply tied to the history recorded in the Orkneyinga Saga.
