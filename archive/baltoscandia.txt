[b]Baltoscandia[/b] or the [b]Baltoscandian Confederation[/b] is a geopolitical concept of a [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states]Baltic[/url]&#150;[url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia]Scandinavian[/url] ([url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries]Nordic[/url]) [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union]union[/url] comprising [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark]Denmark[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia]Estonia[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland]Finland[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland]Iceland[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia]Latvia[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania]Lithuania[/url], [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway]Norway[/url], and [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden]Sweden[/url]. The idea was proposed by a Swedish Professor [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sten_De_Geer]Sten de Geer[/url] in the journal [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geografiska_Annaler]Geografiska Annaler[/url] in 1928 and further developed by Professor [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazys_Pak%C5%A1tas]Kazys Pak&#154;tas[/url], a Lithuanian scientist in the field of geography and geopolitics.
