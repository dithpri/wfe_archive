Sagitta is a dim but distinctive constellation in the northern sky. Its name is Latin for &#39;arrow&#39;, not to be confused with the significantly larger constellation Sagittarius &#39;the archer&#39;. Gamma Sagittae is the constellation&#39;s brightest star, with an apparent magnitude of 3.47. It is an aging red giant star 90% as massive as the Sun that has cooled and expanded to a radius 54 times greater than it. Delta, Epsilon, Zeta, and Theta Sagittae are each multiple stars whose components can be seen in small telescopes.
